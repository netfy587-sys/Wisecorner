How to manage stress with practical, everyday techniques

Stress is the body’s response to pressure. A little can help you focus, but constant stress wears you down. These simple methods help you calm down in the moment and build steadier habits. This is general information, not medical advice.

Common signs of stress

Stress shows up in more than one way.

Body
Tense muscles, headaches, tiredness, stomach upset, poor sleep.
Thoughts
Racing thoughts, trouble concentrating, constant worry.
Mood
Irritability, feeling overwhelmed, low mood.
Behaviour
Avoiding tasks, snapping at others, changes in eating or drinking.

Try each for a week and keep the ones that help.

Daily habits that build resilience

These do not remove stress, but they raise your capacity to handle it.

  • Move your body most days, even briefly.
  • Keep a regular sleep and wake time.
  • Limit news and social media at set times.
  • Spend time with people you trust and talk about what is on your mind.
  • Say no to extra commitments when your plate is full.

When to ask for help

Stress is normal, but it should not take over your life. Talk to a doctor, counsellor or someone you trust if:

  • Stress lasts for weeks and affects your sleep, work or relationships.
  • You feel hopeless, or use alcohol or other substances to cope.
  • You have thoughts of harming yourself. In that case, contact your local emergency number or a crisis helpline right away.

Frequently asked questions

Is all stress bad for you?

No. Short bursts can help you meet a deadline. The problem is long-lasting stress with no time to recover.

How quickly can breathing exercises help?

Many people feel calmer after a few minutes. Regular practice usually makes them work better.
